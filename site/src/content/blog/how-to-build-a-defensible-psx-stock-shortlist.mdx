Having money available does not answer the first investing question. **When will you need it, and
what loss could you absorb without changing that plan?** Those two answers affect whether shares
belong in the plan at all. A shortlist comes later. There is no “safe” PSX stock, and owning several
tickers does not automatically mean the risk is spread.

## Start with the money, not a ticker

Write down the purpose of the money, when it may be needed, and whether a market fall would force a
sale at the wrong time. Risk **capacity** is the financial ability to absorb a loss; risk
**tolerance** is how much fluctuation you can live with. They are not always the same. Both the
[PSX investor guide](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/resources-and-tools/investors/open-an-account-invest)
and [SECP's risk-tolerance explainer](https://www.secp.gov.pk/for-knowledgebase/risk-tolerance/)
put these questions ahead of stock selection.

This is a boundary, not a score from an online quiz. If the money has a near-term purpose or a fall
would disrupt essential spending, do not relabel a volatile share as “relatively safe” to make the
plan work. Compare the available vehicles and, where the choice is consequential, seek qualified
personal advice. This article cannot determine a suitable allocation for an individual.

## Decide whether picking individual shares is the task

A brokerage account gives access to individual PSX shares and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). A fund
can hold a basket of securities, but its label alone does not tell you the risk: an equity fund is
still exposed to equities, while another category may hold a different mix. An ETF may track a broad
index or a narrow industry. Check its underlying holdings, benchmark, fees and trading terms before
calling it diversified. [PSX's ETF guide](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/products/exchange-traded-funds-etfs)
explains the structure and the distinction from an open-ended mutual fund.

## Write a reason for every company

For each possible name, record five short answers. If one cannot be supported, mark it **unknown**;
do not fill the gap with a confident story.

1. **What does the company sell, and to whom?** Check the business description and segment notes
   in its latest annual report.
2. **What changed in revenue, profit and cash?** Compare more than one reporting period and read
   the notes, not only the headline EPS. The guide to [reading PSX accounts](/blog/reading-a-psx-companys-accounts/)
   shows where those statements live.
3. **What could impair the business?** Look for financing needs, customer concentration,
   imported inputs, currency exposure, regulation or a weak balance sheet. The relevant risks
   differ by company.
4. **What does the price already assume?** Compare valuation with the firm's own history and
   business model; a low P/E is not proof of a bargain. See [how to value a PSX company](/blog/how-to-value-a-psx-company/).
5. **Which dated document supports the view?** Use the company's filing or announcement in the
   [PSX Data Portal](https://dps.psx.com.pk/announcements/companies), and note when you checked it.

The output is a research note, not a buy signal. A company can be understandable and still be too
uncertain to put on a shortlist.

## Test the set, not just the names

The PSX's [investor guidance](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/resources-and-tools/investors/open-an-account-invest)
describes diversification across companies, sectors and products. That is a starting principle, not
a guarantee against loss. For a proposed set, make one row per holding and add its sector, main
source of earnings, major exposure and reason for inclusion. Then ask:

- How much of the proposed set depends on the same sector or economic event?
- Do apparently different companies share a financing, fuel, currency or policy risk?
- Would one company or one assumption dominate the outcome?
- Is there a reason for each name beyond “it has been rising” or “someone recommended it”?
- If new results contradict the original case, what fact would cause a re-read?

Two banks can be different tickers yet share exposure to interest rates and credit conditions. Two
manufacturers can share imported energy costs. That does not make either pair unsuitable by itself;
it means the number of tickers understates the overlap. The [sector comparison method](/blog/how-to-compare-psx-sectors/)
helps identify these common drivers. For the macro channels behind a headline market move, read
[what moves the PSX market](/blog/what-moves-the-psx-market/).

## A record you can revise

Keep the shortlist as a dated worksheet: name, primary document, investment question, main
uncertainty, and what would change your view. Revisit it after company results or a material notice.
Do not add a name merely to reach a target count. A smaller list of understood candidates is more
honest than a long list held together by slogans; it is still only a research list, not a portfolio
recommendation.

You can test your understanding in a [practice portfolio](/solutions/psx-research-for-new-investors/)
before committing money. If you are at the beginning of the process, the [PSX starting path](/start/)
puts the broker, vehicle, company and market questions in order.
