The PSX market moves when investors change what they expect about future cash flow, financing cost,
currency risk, regulation or the businesses represented in an index. The useful question is: **what
was the catalyst, how could it reach companies, and what primary evidence would confirm or reject that
explanation?**

A policy announcement can change expectations before it appears in accounts; an earnings release can
show whether it reached revenue, costs, financing or cash.

## 1. SBP policy and interest rates

The first macro channel is the State Bank of Pakistan's monetary-policy stance. A policy decision or
implementation change can be a catalyst because it changes the reference point for short-term
money-market conditions. SBP says its policy rate signals the stance, while implementation uses
open-market operations and standing facilities to steer overnight rates and manage liquidity. See the
[SBP monetary-policy
framework](https://www.sbp.org.pk/our-operations/monetary-policy) and its [monetary-policy
implementation guide](https://www.sbp.org.pk/our-operations/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-implementation).

The transmission is not universal. Funding costs can change for borrowers; banks can see changes in
funding mix and credit demand; companies with material borrowing can face a different finance-cost
burden. A rate change can also alter how investors compare future cash flows with other uses of
capital. These are channels to test, not conclusions about the whole market.

Confirmation needs the SBP material and the relevant company filing for finance costs, borrowing,
interest income, credit quality or cash flow. SBP notes that policy works with a lag, so a same-day
response shows changed expectations, not the full economic effect.

## 2. PBS inflation measures

Inflation is a measurement question before it is a market story. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics
publishes National, Urban and Rural Consumer Price Indices, the Sensitive Price Indicator and the
Wholesale Price Index. PBS explains that it compiles retail and wholesale prices into indices using
approved baskets, weights, cities and markets. The [PBS price-statistics
page](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/price-statistics/) is the starting point for the measure, period and
methodology.

The catalyst may be a new inflation release or a change in its composition. The transmission depends
on the business: higher input prices can pressure margin if selling prices cannot change; household
purchasing power can affect demand; wholesale prices may matter more to an industrial input chain than
a consumer index.

Confirmation means matching the measure to the claimed channel. Record the index and period, then
check the company's results, notes and cash-flow statement for the relevant revenue, cost,
working-capital or margin line. If the filing does not disclose the exposure, its effect remains
unknown.

## 3. FX, imported inputs, energy and regulation

Currency and administered-cost stories often become too broad. A change in the rupee, an imported
commodity, an energy tariff or a regulatory rule matters through a company's actual exposure. The
catalyst may be an official rule, tariff notice, input contract or company disclosure. Transmission
can run through import cost, export receipts, energy expense, inventory replacement cost, working
capital or the ability to pass a cost to customers.

Regulation may change a licence, tax treatment, capital requirement, price mechanism or reporting
obligation. Coverage, timing and company disclosure still matter.

Confirmation should be explicit: identify the affected input or rule, identify the company line that
can carry it, and check the filing or [PSX company-announcements
portal](https://dps.psx.com.pk/announcements/companies). If the source says an exposure exists but
does not quantify its cash effect, report the exposure and leave the magnitude unknown.

## 4. Company earnings and cash flow

Company results are where a market narrative meets reported evidence. An earnings release, audited
statement, reviewed period or formal notice can change the view of revenue quality, margins,
financing, working capital, dividends or cash generation. It can also confirm an earlier macro
catalyst.

State the transmission in accounting terms. Revenue can change because of volume, mix or timing.
Gross profit can change because of inputs and pass-through. Operating cash flow can diverge from
profit because receivables, inventory or payables changed. Finance cost can move because of borrowing
or rates. These lines show what the company reported for a defined period; they do not prove what the
market will do next.

Use the [PSX screener](https://dps.psx.com.pk/screener) to locate a company and the [company
announcements](https://dps.psx.com.pk/announcements/companies) portal to find its evidence. The guide
on [how to value a PSX company](/blog/how-to-value-a-psx-company/) explains why earnings, book value
and dividends answer different questions. Start from the filing period, not a market label.

## 5. What a PSX index actually measures

An index is a measurement layer. The [PSX indices page](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/indices)
describes the KSE-100 as a broad, sector-representative index calculated using free-float
market-capitalisation methodology. A market-wide index can therefore be more affected by companies
with larger eligible representation than by every listed company equally. Other indices use different
rules; the [KSE-100 explainer](/blog/kse-100-index-explained/) covers its construction separately.

The index describes direction and composition; it is not proof that one catalyst caused every
constituent to move. Connect the catalyst to represented companies, then check the filings, notices
or methodology.

## A repeatable checklist

1. **Name the catalyst.** Write the event, source and publication date. If the event cannot be dated, call it unverified.
2. **Name the transmission.** Write one sentence linking the event to a specific rate, cost, revenue, balance-sheet or cash-flow line.
3. **Choose the right measure.** For inflation, record the index and period. For an index, record the methodology and coverage.
4. **Find confirmation.** Check the relevant SBP or PBS release, PSX methodology, company notice or filed statement.
5. **Separate fact from inference.** “The filing reports higher finance cost” is a fact. “The market will rise” is a forecast and is outside this explainer.
6. **Leave gaps visible.** If the source does not disclose the amount, timing or direction of an effect, keep that item unknown.

## Sources and limits

- [SBP Monetary Policy](https://www.sbp.org.pk/our-operations/monetary-policy) — policy framework and the role of the policy rate.
- [SBP Monetary Policy Implementation](https://www.sbp.org.pk/our-operations/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-implementation) — liquidity tools, standing facilities and operating targets.
- [Pakistan Bureau of Statistics: Price Statistics](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/price-statistics/) — price indices, baskets, weights and release categories.
- [PSX Indices](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/indices) — index coverage and methodology descriptions.
- [PSX Data Portal screener](https://dps.psx.com.pk/screener) — company and market lookup.
- [PSX company announcements](https://dps.psx.com.pk/announcements/companies) — the filing trail for listed-company evidence.

This explains channels, not a particular session's cause. It uses no live figures or forecasts and
makes no stock call. Check the latest primary document and period; incomplete evidence stays unknown.
